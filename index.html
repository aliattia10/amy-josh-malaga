Where to eat & drink

Ten traditional, casual places loved by locals — old bodegas, tapas bars, fried-fish counters and beach chiringuitos.

01

El Pimpi

Centro · by the Roman Theatre

Order: Sweet Málaga wine, boquerones en vinagre, jamón

About €18 a head for tapas. It gets busy, so book for a courtyard table.

Málaga dishes to try

Share a plate of each — that's the malagueño way.

Ajoblanco

A chilled almond and garlic soup, often served with grapes — the white cousin of gazpacho.

Porra antequerana

A thick, cold tomato and bread cream from Antequera, topped with egg and ham.

Boquerones

Anchovies, the city's emblem — marinated en vinagre or fried. Malagueños are even nicknamed boquerones.

Pescaíto frito

A mixed plate of small fish, dusted in flour and fried crisp. Best with a squeeze of lemon.

Espetos de sardinas

Sardines skewered on a cane and grilled over embers in sand-filled boats on the beach.

Gazpachuelo malagueño

A warm, comforting fish soup made silky with a garlic-mayonnaise emulsion and potato.

Ensalada malagueña

Potato, salt cod, orange and olives — sweet, salty and very local.

Campero

Málaga's beloved oversized toasted sandwich, piled with ham, cheese, lettuce, tomato and mayo.

Romantic moments

♥

Sunset from Gibralfaro

Climb up to the Mirador de Gibralfaro, next to the castle, in the late afternoon. The whole city, the port and the bullring spread out below as the sky turns gold.

♥

A stroll along Muelle Uno

Walk the palm-lined, pergola-shaded promenade of the port as the boats come in, then carry on to the Malagueta beach.

♥

Toes in the sand at La Malagueta

The city beach is just minutes from the centre — perfect for a quiet walk side by side as the light softens.

♥

An evening in Pedregalejo

Head east to the old fishermen's quarter for a seafront walk, the smell of espetos on the grill, and dinner by the sea.
